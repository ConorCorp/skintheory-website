## The short answer

Sugar and high-glycemic food might make acne worse for some people, but the evidence is mixed. A few small trials found that a low-glycemic diet reduced acne, and sugary drinks are linked to acne in population studies. Other studies found no link. Diet on its own is very unlikely to clear acne, but it can be worth testing on your own skin.

## What the research says

High-glycemic foods are the ones that raise your blood sugar quickly: white bread, white rice, sweets, chips, sugary drinks. The American Academy of Dermatology says [findings from small studies suggest a low-glycemic diet may reduce acne](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/causes/diet), possibly because avoiding blood sugar spikes means less inflammation and oil production. The same page is clear that other studies haven't found a connection, so more research is needed.

The best-known trial is a [2007 randomised controlled trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17616769/) of 43 young men with acne. After 12 weeks, the group on a low-glycemic-load diet had a bigger drop in spots than the group eating a more carbohydrate-heavy diet. But the low-glycemic group also lost more weight, and the authors said more studies were needed to separate the effect of weight loss from the diet itself. Forty-three young men also tells us little about women or older adults.

For sugary drinks, a [2026 study of 819 college students](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42278737/) found that drinking them was linked to higher odds of acne, alongside stress, poor sleep and other habits. A [2026 review of drinks and skin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805319/) agrees that sugar-sweetened drinks are associated with acne in observational studies, while noting that acne guidelines don't make formal dietary recommendations. Associations like these can't prove that sugar causes acne.

## Why it varies from person to person

How your body handles sugar differs from person to person, and so does how much of your diet is high-glycemic in the first place. Cutting sugar from a diet that's already mostly vegetables and lentils changes much less than cutting it from a diet of fizzy drinks and takeaways.

Sugary food also travels with other suspects. Milkshakes and ice cream are [dairy](/acne-triggers/dairy/). Fast food often means late nights, [alcohol](/acne-triggers/alcohol/) or a stressful week. Cravings can spike before [your period](/acne-triggers/period/). If you notice breakouts after a weekend of takeaways, any of those could be the real cause, and only a careful test can separate them.

## Tips while you test

- Change one thing at a time. Cutting sugar, dairy and alcohol in the same week makes a clear result impossible.
- Look for a delay. Breakouts often show up a few days after whatever triggered them, not the same day.
- Don't swap one high-glycemic food for another, like dropping sweets but eating more white bread.
- If you have diabetes or another health condition, talk to your doctor before making big diet changes.
