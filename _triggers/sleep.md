## The short answer

Possibly, for some people. The American Academy of Dermatology lists getting too little sleep as something that can make acne worse, and several studies link poor sleep with more acne. But that research shows associations, there's little direct evidence that sleeping more improves acne, and the link seems to run both ways: acne can also cost you sleep. Tracking your own sleep is the clearest way to see whether it matters for you.

## What the research says

The AAD's page on [who gets acne and what causes it](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/causes/acne-causes) includes "get too little sleep" in its list of things that can worsen acne.

A [2025 review of 18 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41236274/) concluded that acne and sleep are linked in both directions: acne is associated with poor sleep, insomnia and stress, while sleep deprivation may worsen acne through hormones and inflammation. The authors called the link complex and said more research is needed. The review searched only one database, and the studies it covers are mostly observational, so the "sleep worsens acne" direction rests largely on likely mechanisms rather than direct tests.

A [2023 case-control study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36650359/) of 81 people with acne and 76 without found that the acne group had worse sleep quality, and that more severe acne went along with worse sleep, being more of an "evening person", and more anxiety and low mood. A [2026 survey of 819 college students](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42278737/) found poor sleep quality was independently linked to acne, though the effect was modest, and suggested part of the effect of stress on acne might work through worse sleep. The same survey also linked screen time, sugary drinks, smoking and skincare product use with acne, a reminder of how many things travel together.

None of these studies can prove that losing sleep causes acne. They do make it a reasonable thing to test.

## Why it varies from person to person

Sleep rarely changes on its own. A run of short nights usually means something else is going on: exams, a deadline, a new baby, nights out, travel. [Stress](/acne-triggers/stress/), [alcohol](/acne-triggers/alcohol/), late-night [snacks](/acne-triggers/sugar/) and the days before [your period](/acne-triggers/period/) can all come along with bad sleep. And some people simply seem more sensitive to it than others.

That's why it's worth tracking sleep alongside your skin, rather than assuming. A week of bad sleep followed by a breakout might be the sleep, or it might be the stress that kept you awake.

## How to test it on your own skin

SkinTheory helps you get scientific about your skin with your own data, so you can isolate whether sleep is really the cause.

1. **Hypothesis.** Ask something specific, for example "Do I get more spots after nights with less than 7 hours of sleep?" Set it up as a hypothesis in SkinTheory.
2. **Track.** Each entry, take your three skin photos and log skin feeling, mood and pimple count, plus the **Sleep** tracker. Keep your skincare routine and products the same. Mood is worth watching too: if poor sleep and low mood arrive together, you'll see it.
3. **Analyze.** After about 21 entries (3 weeks if you log daily), compare your Sleep log against your pimple count in the graphs, and check whether breakouts follow a day or two after short nights. Scroll the photo timeline for the same period. Acne changes slowly (the AAD says [acne treatments usually take 6 to 8 weeks](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/derm-treat/treat) to show results), so keep going if the answer isn't obvious yet.

## Tips while you test

- Do your night routine before you're exhausted, so tired nights don't also become "skipped routine" nights. Routine reminders in SkinTheory can help.
- Note unusual nights, like travel or illness, so they don't confuse the result.
- If you're struggling with sleep or low mood, talk to a doctor. It matters for more than your skin.

Sleep is tangled up with [stress](/acne-triggers/stress/) and [alcohol](/acne-triggers/alcohol/), so those guides are worth a read too. If you're on a treatment, log sleep alongside it so you know whether it's the [treatment](/acne-treatments/) or your life that's changing your skin.
