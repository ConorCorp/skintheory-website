## The short answer

Benzoyl peroxide is one of the most common acne treatments. It reduces acne-causing bacteria on your skin and is used for mild to moderate acne. You can buy it without a prescription in the US and the UK, usually as a gel, cream, lotion or wash, and a pharmacist can help you choose one. The American Academy of Dermatology notes it tends to work best combined with another acne treatment, which a doctor can advise on.

MedlinePlus says it may take 4 to 6 weeks to see the effects, and to contact your doctor if your acne hasn't improved by then.

## A typical timeline

It varies from person to person, but for many people:

- **First weeks:** dryness, peeling, warmth, tingling or slight stinging are possible side effects. Tell your doctor if they're severe or don't go away.
- **Weeks 4 to 6:** the point where you'd expect to see some effect.
- **Beyond 6 weeks:** if things haven't improved, MedlinePlus suggests talking to your doctor. The American Academy of Dermatology says acne treatments in general take at least 6 to 8 weeks before you see fewer breakouts.

Burning, blistering, redness or swelling where you apply it is a reason to call your doctor. It can also cause a serious allergic reaction in some people: MedlinePlus says to stop using it and get emergency help for hives, throat tightness, trouble breathing, feeling faint, or swelling of the face, eyes, lips or tongue.

One practical thing: benzoyl peroxide can bleach hair and coloured fabrics, including pillowcases and towels.

## What’s worth tracking

- **Progress photos** at the same time and in the same light every day.
- **Pimple count and how your skin feels:** dry, tight, peeling or calm.
- **Side effects you notice,** like dryness and irritation, and whether they ease.
- **When you started,** the product you're using, and any switch to something else.
- **Your routine around it:** moisturiser, sunscreen, cleanser, and anything you've added at the same time. Change one thing at a time if you can, or you won't know which one did it.

A few things make daily photos comparable: the same spot and the same light (a window in the morning, or the same lamp at night), hair back, no makeup, and roughly the same distance from the camera. Take your first entry before you start using it, so day one really is day one. Skipping a day isn't a disaster; the timeline still shows the trend.

## Was it the treatment, or something else?

Benzoyl peroxide is often the first thing people try, so it's also the first thing people give up on. Before you decide it doesn't work, check what else was going on.

## Keep the record

Photos stay private in SkinTheory and never go to your camera roll. Keep your whole history on your phone, so if you move on to another treatment later, you've got a clear record of how benzoyl peroxide went.

If you see a doctor or dermatologist, bring the log. It answers "what have you tried, and for how long?" with photos and dates instead of guesses.

## A note from Conor

Benzoyl peroxide was one of the treatments I worked through for severe adult acne in my early twenties, along with tretinoin, Differin and Accutane. The acne kept coming back and I didn't know why. What finally worked for me was getting scientific about it, and a gentler routine: moisturising more, keeping my skin clean without stripping the oils, and removing products rather than adding them.
